Methodology & What This Does and Doesn't Prove

How the composite score is built

Computed identically across all 17 towns from the National Zoning Atlas's public, independently-collected Jurisdiction Snapshot data — not from our own district-level research, to keep every town on the same consistent scale.

Use Exclusionavg. % of residential land where 2-, 3- & 4+-family housing is prohibited × 0.30
Large-Lot Zoning% of 1-family land requiring ≥80,000 sq ft lots × 0.25
Parking Mandates% of residential land with a minimum parking requirement × 0.15

Renormalized by the sum of weights used (0.70) so the scale stays 0–100.

Why NZA's own data, not our district scorecard

NZA explicitly argues against single composite indices (its own published critique, "It's Time to Retire the Wharton Index") — we agree, which is why each town card below leads with the disaggregated district-level facts, not the composite number. The composite score exists only to let congressional staff triage 17 towns quickly; it is not a substitute for reading the underlying district rules.

Our own district-by-district research (the citations on each card) could not be scored on the same 0–100 scale across all 17 towns — zoning formats differ too much town to town for that to be apples-to-apples. NZA's standardized town-wide rollup can be, so that is what drives the ranking.

Data collection

District-level citations: each town's current zoning regulations were downloaded directly from the town's own website (or, where blocked, a verified mirror/snapshot, flagged on that town's card) and read section-by-section — no values were estimated.

NZA figures: pulled directly from each town's public Jurisdiction Snapshot page at zoningatlas.org using the page's own underlying chart data, not flattened text (which NZA's own charts render in an order that doesn't always match the legend).

Known limitations

  • NZA percentages are town-wide averages, not district-specific — a town's single most-restrictive or most-permissive district can differ from its town-wide number.
  • Several towns' regulations change faster than the Atlas or our own research can track; each card's caveat notes any amendment uncertainty found.
  • A handful of source PDFs were blocked by bot-detection on the town's own site; those cards note the fallback source used and flag it for human re-verification before external use.
